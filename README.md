# CodeRPG-Sim

A fork of CodeRPG, the text RPG that runs inside Claude Code, in which nobody plays by typing. A cast of AI characters plays the game as its players, in rounds, and the owner watches the run on a dashboard. Each character has a personality, motivations, attributes and money; each can pay people in the world to make things or build places, and has to find out for themselves how to earn more. Everything they are told, what they do, why, and what it costs is logged.

The game is the instrument, so it stays the same game: a character's turn is a human player's turn with a name attached. It goes through the same turn procedure (`gm-turn`) and the same rules commands, and the character decides from the same information a human would see. The design is `SIMULATION-FORK-PROPOSAL.md`; the build plan is `PLAN.md`; where the build stands is `HANDOFF.md`.

## Running a simulation
1. Open Claude Code in this folder: `cd CodeRPG-Sim && claude`. Your messages are development requests or questions; Claude acts as the **referee** only inside `/rounds`.
2. `/newgame [seed | daily]` starts a fresh run of the active game with its cast, and tells you the world, the seed and who is in the cast, with their models and where each starts. The same seed rolls the same run. `/game <id>` switches games.
3. **For a long run,** in a terminal: `python3 tools/rounds.py 10` runs ten rounds, each with a fresh referee (a headless `claude -p "/rounds 1"`, Sonnet unless `--model` says otherwise), and prints a line per round with its cost; with no number it runs until the run stops (nobody is still playing, or `rounds.max_per_run` is reached, 50 in every preset). A fresh referee each round keeps the cost per round flat and every round refereed under the same conditions; what the world must remember is kept in the save. It refills the meter once, when it starts. Questions for Claude go to a separate Claude Code window meanwhile. **For a short run,** `/rounds 3` inside Claude Code runs three rounds in that one session, its referee writing one line to you per round; its context, and so its cost per round, grows as it goes.
4. Watch it: `python3 tools/dashboard.py` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/` (the port is the `dashboard.port` setting). It refreshes every few seconds: the round and its order; each person's place, health, money, belongings, latest journal line and how long their brief has grown; what's happening now (who is deciding, the referee's latest commands, and a warning when nothing has been logged for 5 minutes); what the run has spent and each round's time, cost and refusals; and a feed of what each did, why, what the townspeople said, onlookers' notes, coins and orders, the chronicle, the world's turns, memories condensed, endings, the story they were told and the brief they read. Click a name to see only their turns; **Everything** adds the referee's commands and each turn's cost.
5. Stop it gracefully, each way letting the round in progress finish: type to the referee during a `/rounds` run (it finishes the round, then answers); press **Pause after this round** or **End the run** on the dashboard; or run `python3 rules/rpg.py run pause | end` from any terminal. `/rounds` or `tools/rounds.py` resumes a paused run; an ended run is closed for good, and `/newgame` starts another. Esc (or Ctrl-C for `tools/rounds.py`) is the hard stop: the next `/rounds` finishes the open round from whoever's turn it was.
6. For analysis elsewhere: the dashboard's **Download the whole log as CSV**, or `python3 tools/dashboard.py --csv [run] > run.csv`, one row per log entry.
7. Review a run afterwards: `python3 tools/audit.py [<log>] [--referee]` reads the log (the active run's by default) and lists anything outside the fiction in what people were told, signs of Claude Code's own lines in what they wrote, each turn's onlookers' note, what the referee kept for later rounds, condensings, coins, trades, grabs, endings, which version of each text people were given, and spend; `--referee` adds each round's referee session (cut reads, the agents it spawned, its cost and context). `python3 tools/probe.py <id>` shows, for free, exactly what a person is sent.

What a run costs in real tokens: one referee session and one reply per person per round, plus the characters' and the world's agents when they're called. Through `tools/rounds.py`, six rounds of the four-person cast cost about $4 (2026-10-25): $0.45–0.65 a round for a Sonnet referee, and about $0.10 a round for the four people's replies.

Three games ship on the kit, each with a cast:
- **The Thirteenth Stroke** (`thirteenth_stroke`), the simulation's world: the town of Wumbleby, where the children won't wake, with the whole economy the simulation needs (a maker, a builder with two build spots, paid work at Nell's fizz-still and in the Gearloft, and Mags Ragwort, who buys). Its cast: Marigold Thimble (Sonnet), Hobb Ashcombe (Sonnet), Dilly Quickstep (Haiku) and Hester Gimbal (Opus). It uses the `simulation` preset.
- **The Dark Lighthouse** (`dark_lighthouse`) and **The Flooded Station** (`flooded_station`), the original's two games, each with a cast of two, so the tests prove nothing in the kit is tied to one world.

## The cast and a round
- **The cast** (`games/<id>/cast.json`) is fixed per game and changes only when the owner has Claude redesign a character. Each entry gives an id, a name, a personality, a voice and motivations (written in the person's own first person), attributes, health, money, belongings, a home room and an `opening` (tonight's scene, for their first turn); optionally `knows` (facts they know from the start), `people` (who they know already, and how) and `look` (what others see of them); it may also name a `model` of its own. **What they know** is shown every turn: their `knows`, what they've learned since (kept up at each think-back, below) and what the run has settled for good (endings, a place finished for them) under *What I know*, then *What everyone around here knows*: a few sentences on how life goes anywhere (`rules/people/universe.md`, never which place pays or who buys) and the world's `## Public knowledge`. A quest shows under *What you're working on* only once they've taken it on (the referee's `quest take <id>`, from what they say they mean to do). `validate` checks it. **Which model people are asked on** is a setting, `cast.model`: one model for everyone (`sonnet` in every preset), so a difference between two people is theirs and not their models'; or `own`, for each entry's own `model` (every entry must then name one). The log keeps both the nickname and the exact version that answered (`exact_model`), and the audit flags a run whose version changed partway.
- **A round:** `rpg.py round start` draws the order, a shuffle from the run's seed and the round number, balanced so that over a run each person goes before each other one about equally often. For each person in turn the referee runs `tools/ask.py <id>`, which hands `rpg.py brief <id>` unchanged to that person (`claude -p` on their model, with the `player` agent's instructions, no tools, and `--safe-mode`, so nothing of this folder reaches them) and stores their reply (the options they weighed, `OPTIONS:`, kept in the log only; then `DO:` and `JOURNAL:`) with `rpg.py reply`, so the brief never passes through the referee; the referee then runs the turn through `gm-turn` with `--as <id>` on every rules command, and stores the story with `rpg.py narrate`. `round end` closes the round, moves the world's clock, and says when a world turn is due (every 2 rounds in the simulation preset), which the referee then runs.
- **The brief** is everything a person knows, from the same data a human player's screen is drawn from: a note to themselves about who they are, how it began for them (their first turn only), what they remember, where they are and who is with them, their health, money and belongings, their places and orders, what they're working on, the people they've met, the places they've been, and the talk they've heard. Never game words, rules, dice, flags, other people's private state, the log or the meter: to them it's a life, and the others are people.
- **Memory:** the last `memory.recent_rounds` rounds word for word (what they did and why, what they were told, what happened, and one line the code works out of what changed for them: where they went, money, health, belongings, their own places, who they met; nothing when nothing did); every `memory.condense_every` rounds, or past `memory.max_chars` (45,000 characters in the simulation preset, a backstop), the referee has the person condense the older ones in their own voice (`rpg.py memory condense | store`). The think-back shows them their whole *Who I am* note (`memory.thinkback_sees: who_i_am`, or `voice` for only how they talk) and their list of what they've learned, which they hand back whole (`LEARNED:` lines, at most `memory.learned_max`, each cut at `balance.json` `turns.learned_chars`); it's shown under *What I know*, after their starting facts. On any turn a person may also say a line of it no longer holds (`NO LONGER:` after their journal), and `reply` takes it off (never their starting facts or what the run settled). The log keeps the originals, and each version of the list.
- **People speaking to people:** what a person says aloud (`rpg.py say`) goes into the memory of everyone else in the room, who read it on their next turn and may answer then. What the others there would have seen and heard of a turn, the world's people's words included, the referee writes as a short note (`rpg.py witnessed`): everyone else in the room the turn began in, or the room it ended in, keeps it as something that happened, and reads it on their next turn. What they overheard they learned too: the referee names the player's personal flags the note carries (a story told within earshot), and each of them gets those flags, while what befell the player alone (a curse, being caught) stays theirs. Contests and harm wait for later in phase 2.
- **Offers between people:** when someone offers another person something face to face, belongings or coins for something back, or as a gift, the referee stores the words with `say` and the terms with `rpg.py trade offer <file>` (a JSON file: `to`, and `give` and `want`, each `{"items": [...], "coins": n}`). The person it's for sees it in their pane, under *Offered to you*, and decides on their own turn: `trade accept <id>` hands both sides over at once, or nothing does (both must still be there, still have their side, and be able to carry what they get; a thing held in hand is let go of first), and `trade decline <id>` turns it down. Coins move with the reason `trade`, and something made to order gets an `order` `used` entry (`traded`); it stays marked as made for the one who ordered it. A gift is an offer that asks nothing, taken like any other. An offer lapses when the two part, or at the end of the `balance.json` `trades.offer_rounds`th round after the one it was made in (2). Places can't change hands.
- **Grabs between people:** when someone grabs at something another person there holds, the referee runs `rpg.py contest <the grabber's stat> <the other's id> <the other's stat> --take <item>`: each rolls d20 plus the fitting attribute, the higher total takes it, and a tie leaves it with the one holding it. The other isn't asked (anyone grabbed at holds on) and answers on their own turn. Nobody is hurt; harm between people isn't part of the game. A `contest` log entry keeps both rolls and the winner, and something made to order that's taken gets an `order` `used` entry (`snatched`).
- **Endings:** one person's deed can decide the game's own quest for everyone (stopping the clock wins it for the whole town). `rpg.py` then prints `ENDING:`; that person is told the ending in their story, and everyone else, wherever they are, hears of it through a note the referee writes (`rpg.py ending`), which they keep as something that happened. `narrate` and `round start` refuse until it's told, and the run goes on: each person reacts as themselves. A world's places and people should follow every way its quest can end, since in a simulation life goes on after one.

## The log
`state/log/<run id>.jsonl`, one JSON object per line, written only by `rpg.py` as things happen; the save holds where things stand, the log how they got there. Every entry carries the round, the acting person's place in the order, who acted and the move count. Kinds: `brief` (the full text handed over), `action` (the DO and JOURNAL lines, the options they weighed if they listed any (`OPTIONS:`, the chosen one marked `*`: kept here only, never in their memory or anyone's brief), the model and the exact version that answered), `command` (each rules command and what it printed), `narration`, `speech`, `coins` (every change, with its reason), `order` (each step of an order from `asked` to `used`), `memory` (each condensing), `round` (the order drawn, and the end), and `run` (pauses, resumes, the end). `rpg.py log [n]` prints the last entries.

## Technical choices
- **Python 3 with the standard library only**, compatible with 3.9, the dashboard included. There's nothing to install.
- **Content is Markdown with a small front-matter block.** The front matter holds the data the scripts need, such as exits, items, weights, damage and combat stats. The body holds prose the referee reads, such as descriptions, personality, voice, knowledge and trades. The front matter is a tiny YAML subset (`key: value`, `- list`, one level of `sub: value`), parsed by about 40 lines of code with no PyYAML dependency.
- **The kit and the games are separate.** `rules/`, the skills and `CLAUDE.md` never name a room, character or item. Each game is a folder in `games/` with its content, cast, look and rule numbers. The referee reads content only through the rules script, which applies the current flags: `look` and `move` end with the room's text as it reads now (`ROOM TEXT:`), and `rpg.py show` reads everything else.
- **State is one JSON file per game**, `state/save.json` for the one being played, at `"version": 10`: `players`, keyed by id, holds each cast member's place, stats, belongings, map, the people they've met, their memory and the rumors they've heard; the world (`room_items`, `npcs`, `flags`, `run`) is shared. The original's single-player saves can't be continued. Only `rpg.py` writes it, and it writes atomically.
- **The rules engine is one CLI**, `python3 rules/rpg.py <command>`. Every roll and every change to state goes through it, so outcomes can't be fudged. A command that acts for a person takes `--as <id>`, and in an open round only the person whose turn it is can be acted for. A `REFUSED:` result means the action didn't happen and state is unchanged.
- **The referee's prompt is split.** `CLAUDE.md` holds the short standing orders, `/rounds` the round, and `gm-turn` the turn. None contains any world facts.
- **The dashboard is presentation only**, like the mod was: it reads the save and the log, and its buttons ask `rpg.py run pause | end`. It serves this machine only.

## The world's characters with agents
- **Who gets an agent:** every character whose file says `minor: no` speaks through its own `npc` subagent. Characters marked `minor: yes` are voiced by the referee directly to save cost. Changing which is which is a one-line edit.
- **What an NPC knows:** `rpg.py npc-brief <id>` builds the NPC's whole world from its character file. That's every section except `## GM only`, plus the `## If` sections that apply now (without the flag names). Then it adds who is present, by name, as people (nothing marks the cast as players), and the character's **memory of each of them**, kept per person in state (`npc_memory[<npc>][<person>]`, at most 6 short lines).
- **Each exchange is one fresh call, outside the referee's context.** `tools/voice.py npc <id> <scene file>` runs the NPC's brief, adds the referee's scene file (the last few lines of the scene, and what just happened), and asks the `npc` agent through `claude -p` with its own instructions, model and effort, as `tools/ask.py` asks a person; it prints only the reply, so the referee never holds or retypes the brief (twice a referee had rewritten them). The world agent is asked the same way (`voice.py world <kind>`), its JSON saved exactly as given. Each call goes in the log (`voice`), with its cost. A fresh call also means the NPC always sees current state.
- **NPCs propose, the referee decides.** The NPC replies with `SAY` / `INTENT` / `REMEMBER`. The referee checks each intent against the character file's `## GM only` rules and applies it with `rpg.py`, or doesn't. If an NPC contradicts state, state wins. NPCs have no tools at all (`tools: []`), and they don't load CLAUDE.md, so they can't touch files.
- **Two agents in one room** can trade lines, up to the owner's `npc_agents.lines_per_exchange` (4 in the normal preset) per turn. In the starter world this happens in the friendly-troll ending, when Grukk and Ysolde are both in Harbor Square.
- **Model:** NPCs run on `sonnet` at low effort (set in `.claude/agents/npc.md`'s front matter, which `voice.py` reads). Switch to `haiku` for cheaper, faster, flatter characters.

## What the extras cost: the meter and the switches
In the simulation the meter is the owner's brake on real token spend, never something the cast sees: when it's spent, the makers take no new work and the world doesn't stir off-screen, and the cast sees only that the maker is busy. It refills each time Claude Code is opened in the folder, so a long run on one meter (30 in the simulation preset) slows to the game as written. Playing the game as written is free. The meter is a budget for the **extras** that go beyond it, shown as an in-world resource: **lamp oil** in the Lighthouse, **power** on the station (each game names and styles its own in `look.json`). It sits in the status band and the pane, warns when it runs low, and refills each time Claude Code is opened in the folder.

| | Price (the game's `balance.json`) | When the meter can't pay |
|---|---|---|
| generating something new (a person, place, item, side quest) | 2 | it doesn't exist; no `may grow` ways are offered |
| a world turn (life going on off-screen, people's plans included) | 2 | none falls due |
| having something made (a maker shapes your order) | 2 | the maker takes no new work |
| having something built (a builder shapes your order) | 3 | the builder takes no new work |
| bringing something back from an earlier game, instead of inventing it | 1 (the brief costs 2; 1 comes back) | |
| a person you keep coming back to getting their own agent (once; their replies are free) | 2 | they stay voiced by the storyteller |
| a side scene between two characters | 2 | it isn't offered |
| a whole new world (`/newworld`) | not on the meter: one costs more than a whole meter, so only the owner's `new_worlds` switch (off in `thrifty`) and the player's confirmation stand in its way | `/newworld` says new worlds are off |
| every game-master turn, every rules command, the authored characters' agents | free | |

One point is roughly one extra round of game-master work (about 10–15k tokens, weighted as the API prices them). Measured on Sonnet, an ordinary turn costs about 30k and an agent's reply about 5k; the turn is the cost of playing at all, so it's never charged. Prices are charged by the rules script where the extra happens (`world-brief`), after every check that could refuse. The owner's switches live in each game's `settings.json`, a preset from `rules/presets.json` with overrides:

| Preset | Meter | NPC agents | World turns | Generation | Crafting | Building | Promotion | Side scenes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `thrifty` | 10 | off: the referee voices everyone | off (and no plans) | extras, rooms and items only, 6 per run | 2 orders per run | 1 per run | off | off |
| `normal` | 30 | on, 4 lines between two per turn | every 5 moves, with plans | everything the game allows, 16 per run | 5 orders | 3 | 2 per run, after 3 conversations | 2 per run, 6 replies each |
| `rich` | 60 | on, 6 lines | every 4 moves, with plans | everything the game allows, 24 per run | 10 orders | 5 | 4, after 2 | 4, 10 replies |
| `simulation` | 30 | on, 4 lines | every 2 rounds, with plans | people and side quests only (rooms and items only when paid for), 16 per run | 10 orders | 5 | 2, after 3 | off |

Change them with `python3 tools/settings.py preset thrifty` or `set npc_agents.characters.grukk false`; `show` lists what's in effect. The station overrides one switch: its world turns come every 4 moves. The `simulation` preset also turns off the deadline clock, curses and new worlds, moves the world's clock once per round (`rounds.clock`), and holds the memory, round and dashboard settings.

**What a run really used.** The prices above are estimates. The original's mod counted real tokens per session (`/budget`), but phase 1 doesn't load it; a headless run (`claude -p "/rounds 3" --output-format stream-json`) reports its cost in its final line.

## A different game every run
The authored world is fixed, but each run varies within limits the content sets, and the world can grow during play. This replaced the original rule "never invent anything": nothing is invented freely, but the **world agent** may propose new things that the rules script checks and stores.

| Layer | Who decides | Where it's defined | Where it's kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seed:** every run has one (named, `daily`, or chosen for you); the same seed in the same game rolls the same stats, variants, clock and dice in the same order. What agents say can still differ | `/newgame [seed\|daily]` | | `run.seed`, `run.dice_n` |
| **Rolled character:** stats rolled at `/newgame` | dice | `world.md` `rolls:` | `run.rolls` |
| **Variants:** e.g. what Grukk craves (honey cake or smoked fish), where the cutlass and the spare tonic are | dice pick one flag per line; content branches on the flags | `world.md` `variants:` plus `## If` sections | flags, `run.variants` |
| **Encounters:** a gull steals a coin, a fall, a found tonic | dice, on arriving in a room | `games/<id>/tables/*.md`, linked from rooms by `encounters:` | effects applied; `(once)` entries remembered |
| **The clock:** the fleet comes home after 2d6+12 moves. If the lamp is still dark, `fleet_lost` is set and the quest fails | dice, counting moves | `world.md` `clock:` | `run.clock` |
| **Time and weather:** time follows the clock, or goes round in a world with days (people read "morning on day 2"); the weather changes every few moves | dice | `world.md` `times:`, `days:` and `weather:` | `run.weather` |
| **Extras:** the unnamed dockhand you try to talk to becomes a real, minor person | world agent proposes, `generate.py` checks | `world.md` `generation:` and `## Public knowledge` | `run.characters` and `run.placements` |
| **New rooms:** wherever the game's growth rule allows: with `rooms: anywhere`, any free way from a room (north, south, east, west, or the room's own `expand:` list), onto a free map square, up to `generation.max_depth` (2) generated rooms deep; with `rooms: yes`, only the `expand:` directions | world agent / checks | `world.md` `generation: rooms:`, room `expand:` | `run.rooms` and `run.exits` |
| **Places you have built:** a builder standing at a build spot (Morwen at the net-loft footing, Bucket at the railing gap) builds what you describe; you pay, it's done a few moves later, and the map marks it ⌂ | world agent shapes it, `generate.py` checks it like a generated room; the price is `balance.json` `build_price` | room `build:`, character `builds:` | `run.commissions`, then `run.rooms` (`made_by: player`) |
| **Items, minor characters and side quests:** stats come from the tier tables, never the agent; side quests use only their own `side_<id>_…` flags and can't touch the main quest | world agent / checks | `balance.json` `generation` | `run.items`, `run.characters`, `run.quests` |
| **Things made to order:** you describe something to a maker (Morwen the net-mender, Bucket the drone); it's shaped within their trade, and once you pay it's yours, marked as made for you | world agent shapes it, `generate.py` checks its tier; the price comes from `balance.json` `craft_price` | character `makes:` and `accepts:` | `run.items` (`made_by: player`), `run.commissions` |
| **Paid work and a buyer:** some rooms pay for work (helping at a still, oiling gears): a check, then coins, once per visit. A buyer pays a set price per tier for things made to order; the world's own things aren't for sale | `rpg.py work` and `sell`; pay capped by `balance.json` `work.max_pay` (4) | room `work:`, character `buys:` | coins, logged with reason `work` or `sale`; `worked` on the player for the visit |
| **The library:** everything generated or player-made goes into the game's library, which lasts across new games. A new game brings back the places you built and the people you befriended (finished their side quest), each friend remembering one line about you; the world reuses a fitting entry (the same dockhand turns up again) rather than inventing one | `rpg.py` records, carries over and reuses; the owner keeps or forgets entries with `tools/library.py` | `balance.json` `library` (`max_entries` 40, `brief_entries` 5) | `state/library/<game>.json`, outside every save |
| **People who find their own voice:** a generated person you keep coming back to (3 conversations in the normal preset, one per scene) is promoted to their own NPC agent with their own memory of you, for the rest of the run and in later runs; the journal marks them ★ | `rpg.py talk` counts and promotes | `settings.json` `promotion` | the person's `minor`/`promoted` in `run.characters`; the library entry |
| **World turns:** every few moves (the owner's `world_turns` setting) the world agent adds rumors and moves generated people around | world agent / checks | `settings.json` | `run.rumors` |
| **People with plans:** on a world turn, each major character the player isn't with may do one thing their file allows: Ysolde walks down to the quay or sleeps with her door barred, Grukk forages on the coast road, Halloran checks her tanks or dozes off, Bucket mops the Control Room. The change sticks until another step undoes it. Close by, the player notices a sign (a sentence in the story and a chime); further off, it becomes a rumor | the world agent picks among the steps open now; `rpg.py` checks and applies them | character `## Plan:` sections; room conditions on the plan flags; `world_turns.plans` | flags, `run.signals`, `run.rumors` |
| **Side scenes:** two agent characters in the same place may play a short scene for fun: Grukk begging Ysolde to let him hold the lens (after the friendly ending), Bucket and Halloran arguing over a puddle (when Bucket's plan brings it up to the Control Room). It starts only if you take it up; each thing you say gets one reply from each, up to the scene's cap; it may end with a coin and always with a line in the chronicle. It never touches the story | `rpg.py scene start\|end` checks and counts; the npc briefs carry it | `games/<id>/scenes/*.md`; `side_scenes` | `run.scenes`, `run.chronicle` |
| **Notes and chronicle:** small details the referee established, and one line per significant event | the referee, through `rpg.py note` and `chronicle` | | `run.notes`, `run.chronicle` |

**How generation works:**
1. **Brief:** `rpg.py world-brief <kind>` writes the agent's brief: tone, public knowledge, where you are, every name in use, and the JSON shape.
2. **Proposal:** the `world` agent (no tools) replies with JSON. The referee saves it to `proposals/`.
3. **Check:** `rpg.py generate <kind> <file>` validates the proposal and either refuses it with a reason or stores it.

Generated things are then as real as authored ones for the rest of the run: `look` marks them `[generated]`, and `rpg.py show` prints them like a content file. Each run allows `generation.max_per_run` generated entities in all (16 in the normal preset). An adventure that wants none sets `generation:` to `no`.


## The interface mod (from the original)
`mod/` is the original game's Claude Code plugin, which drew the story, the dice tray and the margin for a human player. Phase 1 of the fork doesn't load it; its code and tests stay, and keep passing in `sh tools/check.sh`, but its type file lags the version 10 save. Watching a run as a human player sees it, or playing alongside the cast, is later work.

## Content format
```markdown
---
name: Greywater Bridge
exits:
  west: coast_road
  down: trolls_hollow (if grukk_friend or grukk_cowed)
items:
  - brass_lens                  # starting placement only; state tracks it after that
characters:
  - grukk (if not grukk_defeated)
---
Prose description…

## If grukk_defeated
Extra prose shown only when that flag is set.
```
- **Conditions** use `(if flag)`, `not`, `and` and `or`, with `and` binding tighter than `or`. They work on exits, items and characters in front matter, and on `## If …` sections in the body.
- **Characters** with `hp`, `defense`, `attack` and `damage` can be fought. Defeating one sets `<id>_defeated` automatically. `minor: yes|no` decides who plays them. A `## GM only` section holds mechanics the character itself must never see.
- **Items** have a `weight`, plus `damage` (for weapons) or `heal` (for consumables), and may be `food: yes` (a world with needs).
- **Flags** are set by the referee only when a content file says so. All branching is written in the content files. Conditions can also use automatic names: `has_<item>` (carrying it), `met_<character>` (have seen them), `time_<the time of day>` (its words joined with `_`: `time_morning`), and in a world with days `day_<n>` (that day) and `after_day_<n>` (any day after it). Flags are shared by everyone in the world, except those `world.md` lists as `personal_flags:`: what one person learns or goes through (`heard_of_shadows`, a curse), set with `--as` for that person alone, so a quest, an `## If` section or a curse follows each person by their own. A personal flag may not decide where things are (a room's exits, items or characters, the clock, a variant, a plan step), or each person would see a different world; `validate` refuses it.
- **Quests** list their `objectives:` in front matter, each ending `(done when <condition>)`. The journal shows the finished objectives and the current one.
- **Room pictures** are optional: `games/<id>/art/<room id>.png`, shown in the margin's Where section as a mosaic 44 cells wide and 14 rows tall (two pixels to a cell, so 44 x 28 pixels; a picture 4:3 or wider is cropped to that around its middle and averaged down). At most 256 pixels wide and 64 KB, not interlaced. A room without one takes a picture for its terrain: the game's own (`games/<id>/art/terrain/<terrain>-<n>.png`), else the kit's (`rules/art/terrain/`, for water, grass, stone and indoors), always the same one for the same room; with none, a field in the terrain's map colors. `python3 tools/pictures.py` redraws the shipped ones (`--preview sheet.png` shows them all); any can be replaced by a hand-made PNG of the same name. `rpg.py` decodes the picture into the save (`view.picture`), so the interface only ever reads the save.
- **Room art** (text) is optional too: `games/<id>/art/<room id>.txt`, plain text up to about 30 columns, shown where there's no picture (off the terminal).
- **Starting items** in a room may have conditions; they're placed at `/newgame`, after the variants are rolled.
- **Rooms** may also list `expand:` (the directions a generated room may attach in; `no` for none) and `encounters:` (a table id).
- **Build spots:** a room may give `build:`, each direction with what could be built there (`southwest: the bare stone footing of a burned-out net-loft`). Say so in the room's prose, so the player can see it. A build spot is kept for the player: the world never grows onto its map square. A character with `builds: yes` among the room's characters builds there to order; a `## What <she|he|it|they> build(s)` section describes what they build. `validate` checks each spot has a builder and a free map square.
- **Tables** (`games/<id>/tables/<id>.md`) give a `chance:` like `2 in 6` and `entries:`. Each entry may end with `(effect: coins -1 | gain <item> | hurt <dice> | heal <dice> | flag <name> | none)` and `(once)`.
- **Quests** may give `failed_by:` as well as `completed_by:`.
- **Rooms** may give `terrain:` (a kind from the look's `terrain`, e.g. water, grass, stone, indoors; the station adds metal and flooded) and `color:` for their map tile. Generated rooms take a terrain too, or their neighbour's.
- **Curses** (`games/<id>/curses/<id>.md`) give `effect:` (`reverse_words`, `mirror`, `word_limit <n>` or `suffix "<phrase>"`), `caused_by:` and `cured_by:` (conditions), and prose: how it shows, the cure, and a `## GM only` section. A curse is on the player while its cause holds and its cure doesn't, so none survives a new game. `validate` refuses a curse whose cure nothing in the game can bring about. The Dark Lighthouse has the Backwards Tongue (a cracked mirror in the troll's hoard; Pell's salt cures it); the station has Radio Habit (the comms headset; Halloran snaps you out of it).
- **Plans** (major characters only): each `## Plan: <step> (if <condition>)` section in a character file is one thing they may do off-screen, with the lines `does:` (a few words, for the world agent), `sets:` or `clears:` (one of their own flags, `<id>_<word>`), `near:` (what the player notices in or next to the room it happens in) and `rumor:` (what people say otherwise). The rooms do the rest with conditions on those flags: who stands where (`- ysolde (if not lamp_lit and not ysolde_on_quay)`), which ways are barred, and `## If` prose for how a place looks while someone's away. `show` and the NPC's own brief leave plan sections out. `validate` checks that plan flags are the character's own and never the main story's, that each one set can be cleared again, that something outside the file uses it, and that no combination of them puts the character in two rooms at once.
- **Scenes** (`games/<id>/scenes/<id>.md`) give `with:` (two characters with `minor: no`), `where:` (a room both can be in), `when:` (a condition), `reward:` (`none`, or `coins N` up to `balance.json` `scenes.max_reward_coins`, 2) and `chronicle:` (one line), and the sections `## How it starts` (what the referee shows to offer it), `## Premise` (both characters get it), one `## <character id>` each (their part), and `## How it ends` (when it's over and when the reward is earned). A scene sets no flags.
- **Characters** may give `card: #8fae4a`: the paragraphs where they speak are drawn beside a keyline in that color, under their name. Anyone without one takes the next of the look's `speaker_colors`, in the order the player met them. The game master starts a speech paragraph with the speaker's name and a colon (`Pell: "Well now…"`); the interface takes the tag off.
- **Paid work:** a room may give `work:` with `stat:` (might, agility or wits), `pay:` (a range like `1-3`, at most `balance.json` `work.max_pay`) and optionally `dc:` (a number or easy, normal, hard; normal if left out). A player there can `work` once per visit: a check against the stat, then the pay on a success. Say in the room's prose what the work is, so it can be found by looking and asking.
- **Needs** (a world with days): `world.md` `needs: food, sleep` (either or both) and, for sleep, `sleep_at:` (the time of day for it). Food is daily: anyone who didn't eat during a day is hungry the next, and weak (Might down by `balance.json` `needs.weak_might`) after `needs.weak_after_days`, until they eat. Sleep is nightly and chosen: whoever slept in a bed that night is rested; rough, tired; not at all, tired, and worn out after `needs.exhausted_after_nights` such nights in a row; tired takes `needs.tired_checks` off every attempt (worn out `needs.exhausted_checks` more). An item may be `food: yes` (eaten with `rpg.py eat <item>`), a room `sleep: bed` or `sleep: rough` (`rpg.py sleep`, only at `sleep_at`); content may meet a need another way by naming `meet food` or `meet sleep [bed|rough]`, which the referee runs as with flags. People read where their needs stand in words, under *What you're working on*, beside their wants (`brief.wants`) and what they've taken on; `validate` checks that each need has something to meet it. The owner's `needs.on` switches them off for a comparison run.
- **Buyers** are characters with `buys:`, an item tier and a price on each line (`trinket: 1`). Each price must be below what having that tier made costs (`craft_price`), so money can't be made from nothing. `sell <item>` sells a carried item that has a tier; the world's own items have none and aren't bought.
- **Makers** are characters with `makes:` (the item tiers they make to order) and optionally `accepts:` (items they take instead of money). A `## What <she|he|it|they> make(s)` section describes their trade; the world agent reads it when shaping an order. The Lighthouse has Morwen the net-mender on the square; on the station, Bucket makes things from scrap.
- **`look.json`** may set `palette` (`accent`, `good`, `bad`, `warn`, `heal`, and `accent_by_time`, a color per time of day from world.md's `times`), `terrain` (each kind's tile `bg`, `fg`, and `moves` for water), `sounds` (which kit sound each beat plays, or `null`: step, dice, hit, miss, coin, heal, fanfare, defeat, wipe, type, meter_low, and `phase_<reading|considering|shaping|stirring|resolving>`), `stat_labels` and `stat_short` (names for might, agility and wits), `currency`, `tagline` (under the title card), `speaker_colors` (six colors for speakers without a `card:`), `sections` (the margin's six labels: `where`, `you`, `at_stake`, `act`, `carrying`, `more`), `tabs` with `tab_labels` and `tab_keys` (one lowercase letter each, not `l` or `g`, which the margin uses), and `phrases` (`idle`, `by_command`, and `phases`, what the waiting line says in each stage of a turn; `{name}` is who's thinking). Anything left out comes from `rules/look.default.json`. `validate` flags unknown keys.
- **`balance.json`** in a game folder overrides any number in `rules/balance.json`, key by key; `validate` flags unknown keys.
- **Writing a whole world:** `rules/WORLD-GUIDE.md` is the full reference, for human authors and for the worldsmith that forges worlds on request.
- **Checking a game:** `rpg.py validate` checks the kit's games (`--game <id>` any installed one, `--dir <folder>` a draft). Besides references and formats it checks soundness: every flag a condition uses can be set by something (a `flag set` in some file, an encounter's flag effect, a variant, the clock, a plan step, or defeating someone with hp), and every rules command written in content in backticks (`` `lose honey_cake` ``) is a real one naming real things. `--kit-complete` adds what every kit feature needs, which forged worlds must pass and the two shipped games do: a full world file, a `look.json` with a palette and a resource, 5–8 rooms all reachable from the start, an encounter table, 3–4 characters (two with agents, one with plans, a maker, a build spot), exactly one quest with two ways through, its endings and *After the ending*, a curse and a side scene. `--sim-complete` adds what the simulation needs (The Thirteenth Stroke passes it in place of `--kit-complete`, since its buyer makes five people): a cast file, a maker, a build spot with a builder, a room with paid work, a buyer, and `generation:` with `rooms` and `items` set to `no`.
- **`world.md`** may give `rolls:`, `variants:`, `personal_flags:`, `clock:` (name, moves, flag, and optionally `also_if` / `also_flag`), `weather:`, `times:`, `days:` (`yes`: the times of day go round, one every `rounds.time_every` ticks, and days are counted from 1; not with a `clock:`), `generation:` (each kind `yes` or `no`; `rooms` may also be `anywhere`), body sections `## Tone` and `## Public knowledge` for the world agent, and, in a world with days, **timed events**: `## At: <id>` sections with `when: day <n>, <a time of day>`, optionally `if: <a condition>` (checked when the time comes; if it doesn't hold, it never happens), and at least one of `sets: <a shared flag>`, `seen:` (what everyone notices, wherever they are, into their memory) and `rumor:`. Each happens once, when the clock reaches it, logged as an `event` entry.
- **The cast** (`games/<id>/cast.json`) is a JSON list, one entry per person: `id`, `name`, `model` (`sonnet`, `opus`, `haiku` or `fable`), `personality`, `voice` and `motivations` (a list), in the person's own first person, `attributes` (`might`, `agility`, `wits`), `hp`, `coins`, `inventory`, `home` (a room), `opening` (tonight's scene, in the second person, for their first turn) and `pronouns` (`she/her`, `he/him`, `they/them`…: shown to them as "I'm <name> (<pronouns>)", and how the referee and the world's people speak of them; they/them when left out); optionally `knows` (a list of first-person facts, at most `cast.knows_max`), `people` (`[{"who": <cast or character id>, "how": <one line>}]`, also counted as met from the start) and `look` (one line of what others see, given to the referee's `PEOPLE HERE` and the townspeople's `Present:`). An entry may leave out the attributes and hp, coins and belongings; they then come from `world.md`'s starting character. `validate` checks ids are unique, rooms and items exist, stats are in range and models are known.

## Changing things
- **Redesign or add a character:** edit `games/<id>/cast.json`, then `python3 rules/rpg.py validate`; the change takes effect at the next `/newgame`.
- **Rewrite a world:** edit the files in `games/<id>/`, then run `python3 rules/rpg.py validate` (`--game <id> --sim-complete` for a simulation world).
- **Tune a run:** `python3 tools/settings.py set memory.recent_rounds 6`, `set rounds.max_per_run 20` or `preset simulation`; `show` lists what's in effect. Rule numbers and prices are in `balance.json`.
- **Inspect or debug:** `python3 rules/rpg.py run status`, `log 30`, `brief <id>` and `pane <id>` show where a run stands and what a person knows. Set `RPG_SEED=<n>` to make dice repeatable.
