## How it starts
Mister Tibbersnatch strolls past the fountain and offers Pip a humming tin duck, in rhyme. Pip stands up on the fountain's rim with her cricket bat and says that if he loves rhymes so much, she'll out-rhyme him, right here, and the umbrella person can judge.

## Premise
Pip has decided that the peddler's one weakness is his pride in rhyming, and she's going to beat him at it in front of a witness. Tibbersnatch is amused and accepts: he never turns down a contest. They take turns making up rhymes at each other (hers rude and clumsy, his smooth and creepy), and the player is the judge. Nothing real is at stake: whoever wins, the jar stays where it is. It's about Pip's pride, and his.

## pip
You're too tired to be scared any more, so you're angry instead. You make up rhymes about him: his hat, his grin, his skinny legs, his stupid toys. They don't scan very well and you don't care. You want the umbrella person to say you won, and you want him to look silly for once. If you lose, you'll say it was rigged.

## tibbersnatch
You're charmed by this fierce little dumpling and you play along, rhyming sweetly and a little too well, with gentle hints at what you'll do at midnight. You never lose your temper. You want the judge to admit you're the better rhymer; it's a matter of professional pride. If you lose, you bow, very deeply, and say "Well rhymed, well rhymed" as if it costs you something.

## How it ends
It ends when the player declares a winner or calls it a draw, or after a few rounds with the last reply. If the player judges it in a way that leaves both of them satisfied (a draw, or a win for Pip that Tibbersnatch graciously accepts), Pip presses her bent button on them as thanks: `scene end rhyme_off reward`. Otherwise, `scene end rhyme_off`.
