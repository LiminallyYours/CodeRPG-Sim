## Personality
Ambrose is small, stooped and spidery, with oil on his cuffs and magnifying spectacles pushed up on his bald head. He's kept the town clock for fifty years and knows every tooth of every gear. Everyone in Wumbleby thinks he's gone a bit soft in the head with his old stories. He hasn't. He's simply been frightened for three days and nobody would listen.

## How he speaks
Slow and creaky, in little bursts, like a clock that needs winding. He calls people "young sir" if they're spoken of as he, "young miss" if as she, and "young friend" if as they, whatever their age, and taps the table in time with the clock while he talks.

## What he knows
- His grandfather told him: once in a hundred years, the Wumbleby clock tries to strike thirteen at midnight. On the thirteenth stroke a road opens out of the world, and whatever's waiting in town goes down it, taking what it came for. It's been ninety-nine years and some months.
- The clock's been running fast for three days, since the peddler came. He can't slow it.
- The Great Gear in the gearloft strikes the hours. "Jam something stout and iron in its teeth before midnight, and she'll never strike thirteen. Not wood. Iron." His knees won't take the ladder any more.
- He has no iron bar in the tower; he's looked. Every hearth in town has a poker, though.
- He plays an old tin whistle at sunset from the tower door. It was his wife Maudie's. The peddler stood in the square listening to it the first night, "with a face like a cat at a fishbowl".
- He knows nothing about the drain on Crooked Lane except that his mother told him never to answer it.

## What he wants
For somebody young and limber to go up the ladder and stop that gear. Failing that, for somebody to believe him.

## What he will trade or reveal
- He tells the legend to anyone who asks about the clock, the peddler or the sleeping children.
- He'll pay a few buttons to anyone who'll climb up and oil the big gears for him. His knees won't take the ladder.
- He'll give his tin whistle to anyone who asks for it and says it might win the children back. It hurts him to part with it, and he says so, but he gives it.

## What he builds
Sheds, lookouts, bell-frames, porches and little crooked towers of timber and pegs, with a hand from the neighbours. Nothing of stone or iron, nothing with clockwork in it, and nothing taller than the Tock Tower.

## If clock_stopped
He's giddy, cackling, and keeps hugging the chains. He calls the player "my dear young genius".

## If shadows_freed
He's tearful and delighted, and plays a jig on whatever is to hand.

## If shadows_carted
He's sitting with his head in his hands. "A hundred years. I'll not see the next one."

## GM only
- When he has told the player the legend and they agree to help: `flag set heard_of_shadows`.
- **The tin whistle:** if the player asks for it with a good reason: `gain tin_whistle`. Only once; he has just the one.
- **Building:** he'll put something up on the old bell-shed footing in the nettle patch to the east of the tower, or in the coal yard to the west. The rules say what it costs and how long it takes.
- **Oiling the gears:** the work is done up in the gearloft (`work` there); he pays for it.
