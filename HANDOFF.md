A round holds about 1,000–2,000 characters of memory (the pane is most of it). The fourth session's check lowered `memory.recent_rounds` to 2 and `max_chars` to 3000 in the scratch copy.
- **A person's memory is theirs (JW, fourth session):** the referee stores a condensed memory as given, never judging, correcting or re-asking it (it had re-asked Dilly, whose memory turned a plan to go with Marigold into having gone); remembering an intention as a deed is the bias the self condenser is there to show. - **An ending is everyone's news (JW, fifth session):** one person's deed can decide the quest for all; they hear its ending in their story, the others through `ending <file>`, and the run goes on. A world's places and people must then follow every way its quest ends, since life goes on after one: The Thirteenth Stroke had followed only `shadows_freed`.
- **Pronouns:** `pronouns` in the cast, they/them by default; shown to the referee, the world agent and (since the sixth session) the world's agent-voiced people in `npc-brief`'s `Present:` line. It had been kept out of `npc-brief`, since only cast members carry the tag, and left to the referee to pass on; step 41 showed that drift between rounds (Ambrose called Dilly "young miss", then "young sir"), and JW asked for a fix.
- **What a person is sent (JW, sixth session):** Claude Code's own lines can't be kept out of a `claude -p` call; no proxy (it would rewrite requests made under the owner's subscription login, which Anthropic's gateway docs say to forward unchanged); `/tmp/here` keeps the project and the owner's name out of the path; `probe.py` shows the rest; `audit.py` watches for traces; `player.md` gets no line telling people to ignore anything, unless the audit ever finds a trace.
- **Trades as built (steps 36–37):** an offer is `{"to", "give", "want"}`, each side `{"items": [...], "coins": n}`; a gift asks nothing, a request gives nothing; places can't change hands; one open offer from one person to another, a new one replacing it; an accept checks presence, both sides and the taker's load before anything moves. `balance.json` `trades.offer_rounds` is 2.
- **A fresh referee each round (JW, sixth session):** the people have been fresh calls every turn since phase 1, their continuity their memory in the save; the referee was the one part with a memory of its own, which goes stale and degrades over a long session, so late rounds were refereed under different conditions from early ones. Each round's referee now starts from the save alone, and what it makes up is kept there. The referee, like any game master, sees more than the people (it now sees what each person present carries); what a person is told stays what they could perceive.
- **Asking JW (sixth session):** phrase questions so they stand on their own, without leaning on JW's memory of the project's terms.
- **Contests (JW, seventh session):** turns are one person's at a time, so the only contest is a grab at what another person plainly holds; the one grabbed at holds on unasked (a reflex, not a decision made for them) and answers on their own turn; a tie stays with them; nobody is hurt, and taking things unseen stays out. A staged round (a stand-in for `claude` through `CODERPG_CLAUDE` giving chosen people hand-written replies, the others asked as usual) is how to see something live that people rarely do on their own.
- **Agents outside the referee (ninth session, step 46):** `tools/voice.py` is to the npc and world agents what `ask.py` is to people; the referee writes only a scene file (an npc's two parts under their headings) or a line for the world agent, and never sees or retypes a brief. `--again` re-asks the world agent on the brief already logged, so nothing is charged or ordered twice. A staged round can fix the dice for a check with `RPG_SEED` (one generator per process, so every command's first roll is the same) passed through `tools/rounds.py`.
