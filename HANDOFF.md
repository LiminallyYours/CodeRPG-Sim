## Running it (the owner)
`/newgame [seed]`, then `/rounds [n]`; watch with `python3 tools/dashboard.py` at `http://127.0.0.1:8765/`; stop with the dashboard's buttons, `rpg.py run pause | end`, or by typing to the referee (each lets the round finish). The README's *Running a simulation* has it all. Two things to know before a long run:
- **The meter** (the owner's brake on extras) holds 30 in the simulation preset and refills only when Claude Code is opened; world turns alone (2 points every 2 rounds) spend it in about 30 rounds, after which the world stops stirring off-screen and the makers take no orders. Raise `meter.capacity` with `tools/settings.py` for long runs.
- **Cost seen:** the end-to-end run (four people, Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, a Sonnet referee) cost about $4.10 for 19 turns; the condensing check (two people, Haiku and Sonnet, 4 rounds, two condensings each) $2.02; the referee's context grows over a run, so later rounds cost more than early ones.

## Decided along the way
- Claude Code's agent list labels `tools: []` agents "All tools"; a live test (2026-10-24) showed they get only the hand-back tool. Trust the test, not the label.
- **Step 5 as built:** what moves the world's clock is a setting, `rounds.clock` (`move` or `round`), and the existing `world_turns.every_moves` counts ticks (rounds in the simulation preset, where it is 2). `PLAN.md` was updated to say so. `clock.on` and `curses.on` are enforced; `memory.*`, `brief.dice_lines` and `dashboard.port` are stored for later steps.
- **Step 6–7, how the engine knows who acts:** `ACTOR` (from `--as`, taken from any position in `main()`) and `me(state)`, the acting player's record; `with acting(pid):` reads the save as another player (the view, NPC briefs, finished side quests). Commands about the installed games (`WORLD_COMMANDS`: game, validate, settings, meter, forge) ignore `--as`; `newgame` and `game switch` end with the first player's look.
- **Cast entries may leave out** attributes and hp, coins, belongings and the weapon in hand; those then come from `world.md`'s starting character (rolled as before). Each shipped game's first cast member does this, so its seeded tests keep their dice. The Thirteenth Stroke's two-person cast is a **placeholder** until step 15.
- **The test helper** `rpg()` acts as the save's first player unless told otherwise (`as_="corin"`, or `as_=False` for none).
- **The `npc` agent's instructions** no longer say "the player" (they say "the person talking to you"): with several people present it was ambiguous, and it marked who was a player. Its dry run (step 8, Ysolde with Wren and Corin, two real spawns) kept the two apart and stayed in character.
- **Not yet per player:** `run.signals` (the mod's chime list) is shared, though what each player notices now goes into their own memory; the mod's type file lags the version 10 save (the mod is unused). World turns between rounds are no one's (`NO_ONE_COMMANDS`), and their brief lists where every player is.
- **Steps 11 and 12 were swapped** (memory first, since an order's `asked` entry needs the turn's own words). `reply <id> <file>` and `narrate <id> <file>` read files in `proposals/`, not shell arguments (quotes and apostrophes). In an open round only the player whose turn it is can be acted for (`whose_turn`); referee commands (`REFEREE_COMMANDS`) name the player themselves.
- **The log** is held in memory and written with the save (`flush_log`), so a refused command leaves only its own `refused` command entry. `coins` takes a reason (`COIN_REASONS`); content and generated text name it.
- **Orders** carry ids `o1`, `o2`, ... from `world-brief craft|build`; `asked` carries the player's DO and JOURNAL from this turn. Every built place is its owner's stash in phase 1 (`stash_owner`).
- **The brief opens with "Who you are"** (personality, voice, motivations from the cast entry); the proposal's list left it out. Each round's memory keeps the pane as it stood after the turn ("How things stood after"), as the proposal says; it is the bulk of a brief's size.
- **Steps 15–17 as built (2026-10-24):** work is `work:` on a room (`stat`, `pay` as a range up to `balance.json` `work.max_pay` 4, `dc` default normal); the player's `worked` holds the room worked this visit and `move` clears it (no `STATE_VERSION` bump: read with `.get`). A buyer's prices must be below `craft_price`, and only items with a tier sell. `look` prints `WORK HERE:` and `BUYERS HERE:` lines for the referee. Content may write `` `sell <item>` ``: an argument in angle brackets is a placeholder to the soundness check. The Thirteenth Stroke has five people with Mags, so `test_rules.py` holds it to `--sim-complete` (`SIM_GAMES`) instead of `--kit-complete`.
- **JW's decisions after steps 15–17 (2026-10-24):** The Thirteenth Stroke's `settings.json` uses the `simulation` preset. The cast's personality, voice and motivations are written in the person's own first person, and the brief opens with them as `## Who I am (a note to myself)`; the world's telling stays second person (proposal 4.1).
- **Run control (approved by JW 2026-10-24, recorded under step 19 in `PLAN.md`):** the engine side is built: `rounds.max_per_run` (50 in every preset, 0 for no limit), `rpg.py run status | pause | resume | end`, and `round start` refusing while a pause or end is asked or the limit is reached (the round in progress always finishes). Step 19 adds `/rounds [n]` (which runs `run resume` to go on) and the standing order that an owner's message mid-run means "finish this round, then stop and answer"; step 20 adds the dashboard's Pause and End buttons.
- **The `player` agent can't be spawned in the session that created it** (agent files load at session start). It was checked headless instead: `claude -p --agent player --model haiku "<brief>"` in a scratch copy replied in exactly the two parts.
- **Step 18 as built (JW's decisions, third session, 2026-10-24):** no `journal` command: `reply` already stores the JOURNAL line with the DO line, and the procedure acts on the DO line only (the journal is private: never acted on or passed to NPCs). `look` names the other cast members present (`PEOPLE HERE:`). `say <file> --as <id>` puts words spoken aloud into every other cast member's memory in the room as `told`, so they read them in their next brief and may answer on their turn (proposal 6.5, built now); a brief keeps the opening until a player's first turn, even with words already heard. `gm-turn` starts every turn with `look` (the "a plain move skips the look" shortcut went: others have acted since). Handing things to another cast member, trading or harming them is treated as refused until phase 2. `/newgame` narrates nothing and reports world, seed and `CAST:` to the owner, and points to `/rounds` (step 19).
- **Step 19 as built:** `CLAUDE.md` now says the owner's messages are development or questions and the referee acts only inside `/rounds`, with six standing orders; `DEVELOPING.md`'s start note matches. The dry run ran headless: in a scratch copy, `claude -p "/rounds 3" --model sonnet --settings .claude/settings.json --output-format stream-json --verbose` in the background (about 4 minutes and $1.16 for 2 players and 3 rounds); the log's `brief`, `narration` and `speech` entries were checked against the tests' forbidden-word pattern and read by hand. An untrusted scratch folder warns that its `permissions.allow` entries are ignored, but the commands still ran.
- **Step 20 as built:** one page and `/api/live` (JSON), served on 127.0.0.1 only and refreshed every 3 seconds; it reads `state/save.json` and the run's log, never writes either. Pause and End `POST` to `/api/run/pause|end`, which run `rpg.py run pause|end`; they need the page's `X-Dashboard: 1` header (a page elsewhere can't send it without a preflight, which is never answered), and every request needs a `Host` of `127.0.0.1` or `localhost`. Resuming stays with `/rounds`. For a screenshot, Brave (Chromium) runs headless: `"/Applications/Brave Browser.app/Contents/MacOS/Brave Browser" --headless=new --screenshot=<png> --window-size=1280,1700 --virtual-time-budget=4000 <url>`; below about 500px wide it doesn't lay out a narrower window, so check phone width inside a 390px iframe.
- **Curses follow shared story flags,** so in a world with several players one person's curse would fall on everyone; that's why the Drain-Echo was removed rather than curses switched on. Per-person curse flags would be the fix, if curses come back.
- **After step 19's dry run (JW):** what a player notices close by (a world turn's `SIGNAL`) goes into their memory as `seen`, not a one-off "Just now"; a player hears the talk going around only when the world's people are with them, at their brief or `look` (`overhear`), and their pane shows only what they've heard (`heard`). The original's margin showed every rumor, but it had one player.
- **Step 21 as built:** `csv_of` in `tools/dashboard.py`: the fields every entry has (`kind`, `t`, `round`, `order_position`, `player`, `move`), then every other field in name order; lists and objects as JSON.
- **Step 22 so far:** README and DEVELOPING rewritten for the fork (the original's world mechanics and content format kept; the mod reduced to a note). The end-to-end dry run (full cast, seed `dry-2`, `/newgame` then open-ended `/rounds`, Pause pressed through the dashboard's API at round 5, a 6-round cap as a backstop) is recorded under step 22 in `PLAN.md`. Its fixes, a commit each: `/rounds` never stops early and gives no account of anyone when it stops (the referee had stopped after 2 rounds "to check in"); the player agent says what they do in a few sentences, with `turns.do_chars` raised to 1200 (Hester, on Opus, wrote 873); a finished building goes into its owner's memory; the Drain-Echo is gone from The Thirteenth Stroke.
- **Driving a dry run:** a small Python watcher that polls the scratch log and `POST`s `/api/run/pause` with `X-Dashboard: 1` stands in for the owner's button; keep a `rounds.max_per_run` cap in the scratch copy as a backstop, since an open-ended `/rounds` otherwise runs to 50.
- **The tests' `FORBIDDEN` pattern** catches ordinary English in a person's own words ("I can check where…" in a journal line, kept in memory): read each hit before calling it a leak.
- **Condensing comes due by the size cap only past `memory.recent_rounds`:** `condensing_due` needs both more rounds held than are kept and the text over `memory.max_chars`, so lowering `max_chars` alone does nothing before round 6. A round holds about 1,000–2,000 characters of memory (the pane is most of it). The fourth session's check lowered `memory.recent_rounds` to 2 and `max_chars` to 3000 in the scratch copy.
- **A person's memory is theirs (JW, fourth session):** the referee stores a condensed memory as given, never judging, correcting or re-asking it (it had re-asked Dilly, whose memory turned a plan to go with Marigold into having gone); remembering an intention as a deed is the bias the self condenser is there to show. Each remembered stretch is its own paragraph in the brief.
